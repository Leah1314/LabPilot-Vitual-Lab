## **📩 Submissions**

You must submit your projects by 3:30 PM latest via a Devpost. Submission link: https://daytona-hacksprint-sf-jul-2026.devpost.com/

Make sure your submission includes:

- Team name: Pick something unique!
- Team members: List everyone on your team. (Ensure your Devpost includes emails and socials so we can contact or tag you!)
- Demo video: A short screen recording (under 2 minutes) showing what you built is highly recommended.
- Project description:
    - A brief 2–3 sentence summary of your project
    - The problem you’re solving and its impact
    - Key technical architecture and components
    - A list of all sponsor tools/protocols used and how you integrated them
- Public Github repo URL

## **📝 Judging**

Judging proceeds in two rounds:

- **First Round:** The first round of judging will be based on the Devpost submissions. In total, eight projects will be chosen to pitch for the final prizes. Detailed judging guidelines will be shared on Tuesday**,** prior to project submissions.
- **Final Round:** The top **eight** teams in ranking will get to demo on stage to a panel of judges.
    - Each team will have 3 minutes to pitch, followed by 2 minutes for Q&A.
    - After all presentations, the final round judges will deliberate and select the top 3 winners.
    - Partners will select the **Best Use Awards**, which will be presented during the awards ceremony. Please note that partners may select winners from **all participating teams**, not only the finalist teams that advanced to the pitch session for the overall Top 3 prizes.

Judging criteria:

- **Impact Potential (25%)** – Does the project address a real problem? How significant is the potential impact on users or industries?
- **Technical Execution (25%)** – How well is the project built?
- **Creativity (25%)** – How original or innovative is the idea?
- **Presentation (25%)** – How effectively is the project communicated and demonstrated?
- **Sponsor Tool Usage (Bonus)** – How well are sponsor tools integrated into the project? Is the usage meaningful and aligned with the project’s goals?
